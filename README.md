# The Arc

Open-world first-person survival / holdout game (Three.js, plain static files — no build step for Vercel).

- `game.js` is generated: edit the parts in `src/` and run `./build.sh`

  - `1b-build.js` terrain chunks, water, forest, grass, mist

  - `1f-houses.js` (Nov 10 rebuild) Kestrel's buildings, made the way settlers would: a frame, cladding a board or a tin sheet at a time (each its own colour, a little out of true, patched), windows with frames, glass, curtains, shutters or boards, doors left ajar, overlapping roof sheets with a tarp and tyres over the leak, gutters into rain barrels, stovepipes, aerials, upstairs rooms with balconies, laundry and stairs up the back, lean-tos of firewood; inside, lined walls, a board ceiling, shelves of stock and each trade's tools (Bryce's prop models). Its own random stream, so editing a house never moves anything else.

World: ~4.4 km across, one forest biome. The Chapter 1 valley sits in the middle inside a ring of ridges; roads leave it through passes
to the outlands (Varnhold, Sunscar, Elderhollow clan holds, Cinder City, the Watchers, Lake Varr, Mirrorwater). Terrain chunks and
outland forest cells stream in around the player (`updTerrain` / `updForest` in `1b-build.js`).

Kestrel: the wall is 64 m out. The Arc (the main hull) is walk-in — E at its door seals/opens the blast doors; a ramp inside leads to a catwalk with a firing slit over the yard. Raiders who get over the wall go for the Arc: if it's sealed they hammer the doors, if they get in they go for the core. Settlers shelter inside at night. Main Street runs from the gate to the Arc's door; Arc Row, Cross Street, West Lane and East Lane split the yard into blocks, and each building faces a street. Seven shops, each with a keeper (E: trade for scrap, Q: their extra — Kade upgrades, Juno patches, Rook daily ammo) and a station at the side (cook, craft + forge, materials, medicine, weapons, clothing). Gus at Salvage pays 75% for what you sell (everyone else 50%). The Bunkhouse lets you rest to full health. Shop stock refreshes daily and better gear appears as your tier rises. Six named settlers wander the yard and barter what they've gathered.

Start: you wake at the dropsite (south-west, ~400 m out) beside a section of the Arc torn off on the way down — loot the wreck, then follow the old road north-east to Kestrel, or dig in and build there. Kestrel's night raids start once you've reached it.

The Arc (Nov 10): the hull is dented, wavy and patched, with bent and snapped ribs; its east end carries on through a buckled joint into the east wing, which bends round to the south and is walk-in too (`WING`, `inWing`, `hullOff` in `1c-camp.js`). The streets and buildings sit a little off square.

The Rust (red fog, Nov 10): the first one comes ~45 s after you first reach Kestrel (day 1), then every 7–10 days. The fog clears inside the hull. It hurts more the longer you're out (~5 hp/s rising to ~15). A roof does not stop it: only the sealed hull counts as cover (out in the world: gas mask, hazmat, breather, Rust medicine). Everyone in town runs for the Arc; one or two get caught, go down, and die ~70 s later unless you treat them (E, uses a bandage / medkit / Rust medicine). `fogNPC` in `3-game.js`.

Night 100 is announced over the radio: from day 70 Farlight (and once Halvard) call Kestrel as the clans gather (`RADIO_CALLS` in 3-game.js). Carry a radio — take a walkie-talkie from the rack in the Arc's radio room (end of the east wing) — and you hear each call at dawn; otherwise they wait on the radio desk in the Arc (E to answer).

Open world: no chapters. Tier keys: clan insignia (2), Arc ID chip (3), Greyhold card (4), Dr. Arden's notebook (5), Gate Stone codes (6). Nights bring raids (clans, then Ironjaw from night 6, Greyhold from night 20); night 100 is the big one. Dying drops your bag where you fell.

Movement has momentum (accelerate/brake over a fraction of a second, slide along trees, steep climbs slow you), jump buffering + coyote time, and a stamina-limited sprint (bar above health; run it dry and you're winded until a third comes back).

Settings (Nov 10): title menu → Settings, the Settings tab in the bag, or O. **Textures** Low / Medium / High (`TEX_LEVELS` in 1-world.js: model texture cap 512 / 1024 / full, sharpness, phone or PC ground textures; applies on reload, the game saves first). **View distance** Near 300 m / Medium 450 m / Far 700 m / Max no limit (`VIEW_LEVELS`, `setViewDist` in 3-game.js; live): a fog wall closes everything in at that distance (packed into `fogNear` — see `packFogNear`), and terrain chunks, forest cells, props, districts, far forest and smoke past it aren't streamed or drawn; the 9 km horizon ranges only draw at Max. Saved in localStorage `arc-gfx`; `?tex=` / `?view=` in the URL override. Defaults: PC High + Far, phones Low + Medium.

Performance: hand-placed props are distance-culled by size (`buildCull`/`updCull` in `3-game.js`, hidden ones go to layer 1 so neither the camera nor the shadow pass draws them); tiny props don't cast shadows; resolution adapts to hold frame rate (`adaptRes`).
More performance: `bakeWorld` (3-game.js) merges static props that share a look into one mesh per group (~1,900 fewer draw calls; anything that moves, hides or flickers is skipped, and the wall bakes itself in `buildWall`). The point lights stay in the scene but invisible, and a pool of 4 real lights copies whichever ones reach the camera (`buildLightPool`/`updLightPool`) — the light count is fixed, so no shader recompiles. People swap to `*-lo.glb` (~1.5k triangles) past ~25 m, and enemies carry the low-poly guns. Deer/boar parts are fused per moving piece. Tone mapping happens in the grade pass (no OutputPass), and `adaptRes` drops bloom if the floor resolution still isn't enough.

People (Nov 10): Bryce's **Keeper foot soldier** (`assets/models/keeper.glb`, from ELYRA) is really rigged — his own skeleton and Walking/Running clips, played off the stride phase (`rigCopy` / `poseWalk` in 2-actors.js; standing, the walk is held where the feet pass), with `keeper-lo.glb` (3k, arms down) past ~25 m. Kestrel's watch is five of them: two on the gate, three walking the streets, each with a rifle in his right hand; he's also in the yard extras. Bryce's **settler** (ELYRA's Keeper civilian, baked standing with her arms down: `settler.glb` 8k / `settler-lo.glb`) joins the civilians: shopkeepers, settlers and extras pick from both. Barracks guards are Keepers too.

People: the survivor models face their own +x, so `makePerson` turns them to face +z (the way they move). They walk with legs/arms swung in the vertex shader (`walkify` / `walkGeo` in 2-actors.js — each triangle is assigned to a side so fused legs part cleanly). Settlers and yard extras walk the street network (`WALK` in 1c-camp.js: junctions, street ends and a spot in front of each shop; `WALK.settle()` nudges nodes off anything solid once the town is built).

Audio on phones: `navigator.audioSession.type = 'playback'` (iOS ringer switch), the context + both music players unlock on touchend/pointerup/click/key anywhere, and the music pauses/resumes with the tab.

HUD: health (bottom-left), toolbar 1–5 (bottom: 1–2 guns, 3 melee/bow, 4 eat, 5 treat), gun + ammo (bottom-right), minimap (top-right, tap for the full map), bag (inventory).

Controls — PC: WASD, hold Shift to run, C crouch, Z companion order, mouse, click fire/swing, right-click aim, R reload, 1–5 toolbar (Q / wheel cycle weapons), E use, Q next action / dismantle, G throw, B binoculars, Space jump/swim, I bag + craft, M map, N sound.
Secret: tap the heart (health bar) 7 times fast, or type "floombus" — every item in the game, a bottomless bag, all tiers, and a FLY button under the bag (Space/JUMP up, C down). Do it again to top up. Controls follow the device (phones/tablets: touch; computers, including touchscreen laptops: keyboard + mouse).
Phone: left thumb move (push to the edge, or tap RUN, to run), right thumb look, crosshair button fires, AIM; tap toolbar slots, the minimap, or the bag.

Nov 10 (later): a countdown to the next red fog sits top-middle (`fogCountdown` in 3-game.js). When the fog arrives the Arc's blast doors shut themselves once whoever is at them is through (or after 8 s), and open again when it lifts (`sealForFog`); E at the door still works. Townsfolk, keepers and the watch (the two on the gate too) take the streets to the Arc (`fogRouteFor`) and anyone left outside gets hurt. The settlement gate opens/shuts with E, and a shut gate stops you too. Aiming works like ELYRA: the gun stays at your side and the view zooms (ARs 2x, pistols/SMGs/shotguns/bows 1.3x); only the hunting and sniper rifles scope in, 4x then 7x with Z (phone: ZOOM). No bag button: E (phone: USE, which reads BAG when nothing's near) opens the bag. The pause button top-left (or Esc / P) stops the game and holds Map, Sound and Main menu.

## Gentler survival (Nov 11, Part 3)

Survival backs up the adventure instead of interrupting it. Tuning knobs: `SURV`, `BUFF_DEF` and `DUR_K` in `2a-data.js`; logic in `updSurvival` / `repairGear` (2b-craft.js).
- Food drains ~3× slower than before (~65 min of walking from full). Starving wears health down to 12 and stops, it never kills. Natural healing starts at 35 food.
- Water is its own light bar (~45 min from full, saved with the weather): drink at any lake or river ("Drink", small chance of an upset stomach) or from water you carry (slot 4 drinks first when you're thirsty). Running dry never hurts — it just stops natural healing and halves stamina refill until you drink.
- Cooked food gives buffs: **Energised** (fish, bread, clean water: sprinting costs half, stamina refills faster), **Mending** (stews, soups, honey, roast: heal over time), **Hardy** (cooked/dried meat, roast, stew: −20% damage, a bit of red-fog resistance). Raw food is less risky and sickness is shorter.
- Fishing: better odds, a rod can land two, fish bite more in rain and fog. Water: rainwater is clean, rain fills a carried pot or canteen, purification tablets craft from charcoal + herb, rainwater filters without charcoal.
- Gear lasts 2.5× longer (old saves' gear is stretched once on load). Repair from the bag (per item, or the **Repair all** button by the gear list, most worn first — both only appear once something is below 40% condition): a repair kit fixes fully anywhere; otherwise a few scrap (or a stick + 2 fiber for primitive tools) patches to 60%, or fully at a workbench/forge. You get a warning when something is wearing out.
Exploration (Nov 11, Part 1): Millbrook (walked-out farm village; the Rourke cellar under the bottom house, with a hidden room behind a shelf), KVRN relay station on the south rim (the numbers tape), Kestrel Ridge trail (cairns from behind the camp to the summit, then down to Whisper Cave — a painted hall, an underground pool, a floor that gives way into the deep chamber with the thirteen marks), the Coldwater Line (a 240 m rail tunnel through the east rim: west portal in the valley, east portal outside; fuse in the alcove powers the maintenance bay), Fort Hesper (old army post, Ironjaw squatters, respawns like the other bases; the bunker under it: power the generator, the colonel's drawer has the armory key and the Brightwater keycard), Brightwater Annex (the lab under it: decon, specimen labs, a hot room, the archive behind the keycard), the Long Fall (Hydroponics Ring C, crashed decades ago; the crew locker's ID chip opens the med stores), Route 9 (a jammed highway with a chained trailer and a fallen overpass), Ember Rest (a burnt clan camp), the Black Door (SITE 9, AWAITING SIGNAL — not openable yet), eight little camps, six marked stashes, the rope bridge and the stepping stones. 39 logs in all; the Journal tab lists what you've found and which places are still out there.

## Cinder City (Nov 11 — overhaul part 2)

From Bryce's overhaul plan (§1): the dead city is now somewhere to explore, not just loot from the street. Code: `src/1j-city-interiors.js`, plus small changes in `1e-city.js` and five hook calls in `3-game.js` (`cityNear` in INTERACT, `cityCollide` after `collide`, `cityGround` after `floorAt`, `updCity` in the frame loop, `cityAfterWeather` right after `updWeather`: no rain inside the buildings, and underground no sun, a dim fill and a dark haze, the way Part 1's pockets are lit).

**Places** (`citySites()` picks blocks off the city grid; same blocks every load):
- **Hollis Hardware** (the edge facing Kestrel): a shop with aisles, an upstairs flat, a cellar. A note upstairs sends you to Mercy General for the shelter code. Under a crate in the cellar is a hatch down to **Shelter 12**, a pre-war bunker behind a keypad blast door (code `4177`, once you've read the card at Mercy). Inside: Shelter 12's stores (OP table), a gun safe, a terminal.
- **Mercy General**: four floors of wards, a triage terminal, the nurses' desk card (third floor), a weak floor, a helipad roof with a wrecked medevac drone (Arc loot, medkit + IV), and **the morgue** in the basement (drug safe: medkit, antibiotics, surgical kit).
- **Precinct 9**: offices, lockers, holding cells, a duty terminal, a sniper's nest on the roof (military loot), and a **boarded-up armoury** on the 2nd floor (crowbar).
- **Cassel Municipal** (offices): a mail terminal, a weak floor, and a boarded-up records room on the top floor.
- **Five apartment blocks**: one with a **rooftop garden** (a quiet place, gardener's chest with seeds), one with a **survivor's camp** on the roof (cache under the water tank), one with a sniper's nest, two with **rooms hidden behind a shelf** (push it aside), cellars, and fire escapes (E at the foot or the top).
- **Kessler St.** station: a ticket hall, then stairs down to the platform and **Line 2**, which runs east under the city and comes up through a manhole **inside the Foundry's wall** (works both ways: a way in that skips the gate). The gate past the platform needs power: put 1 fuel in the generator (a note on the platform says so). On the way: a bricked-up branch with something scratched on the far side (a lead for later), a radiation leak, a cave-in to squeeze past, a maintenance locker (bolt cutters).
- **Cinder No. 4**: the power plant. A cracked containment dome, a cooling-tower stub, green water, a fenced compound, a control building (control-room safe: OP loot + shielding + a power cell; electronic bypass). **Radiation** everywhere inside the fence.
- **The Glasshouse**: an overgrown conservatory with a pond and a lemon tree. **Quiet**: Ironjaw won't follow you in, and you heal.

**How the buildings work.** Each is put together from parts (`hkit`, one mesh per material per building): a raised ground floor (so grass never grows through it) with steps and a ramp up to the door, storeys 3.4 m apart, windows (glass, boards, blown out), partitions with doorways (three columns of rooms, front and back), and a stair: one ramp per storey stacked against the back wall behind a stairwell wall, up to a bulkhead on the roof. Floors are walkable `FLOORS` rects with the stairwell (and any weak patch) cut out. The insides only draw within ~90 m (`CIN_G`).
- **Walls you bump into are y-aware** (`CWALLS`, `cityCollide`): a wall on the 2nd floor doesn't stop you on the roof, a sill stops you at a window, the parapet keeps you on the roof. Outer walls also get ordinary column colliders so people, animals and bullets respect them.
- **Underground** (basements, Shelter 12, the Line) is reached through hatches/stairs/ladders with a short fade (`cityGo`), then walked for real: while `P.under` is set, `UNDER` rects are the ground (`cityGround`). It only draws while you're down there (`CUN_G`). Ironjaw up top lose you while you're underground. (Part 1's pockets, `UG`, are a separate system out past the world's edge; the two never overlap.)
- **Weak floors**: patched boards over a hole. Stand on them and they sag and creak; still on them ~0.4 s later and they go, dropping you a floor (a little damage). Sprint across and they hold. One drops you straight into a hidden room. Fallen floors stay fallen (saved).
- **Radiation** (`CZONES`, kind `rad`) fills the same radiation meter as Part 1 (`P.rad`: the bar bottom-left, wears off, Juno or Rust medicine clears it), but fast; past 60 it hurts, up to ~6 hp/s when it's full, so Cinder No. 4 can kill you. Suits and masks slow it the way they slow the Rust fog (`fogResist`). Geiger clicks and a badge show how hot it is.
- **Quiet places** (`CZONES`, kind `quiet`): no one chases you in, you heal (~2.5 hp/s if you're fed), birdsong.
- **Records**: 19 notes, terminals and recordings (`CLORE`) tell what happened: the burning, the Greyhold list, the Grey Door, Officer Dane, the reactor, and a broadcast from the Mountain (Season 2 lead). They open in Part 1's reader and file into the **Journal** (as `city_*` entries in `LOGS`, grouped by place).
- **Loot**: every stash inside is a normal cache with its own id (`cc_*`), so refills and saves work as before. Each building has 2–3 ordinary ones plus its special ones.

**Saves**: everything the city remembers is in `G.flags.city` (`open` doors/hatches, `coll` fallen floors, `power`, `lore` read). Old saves just start with nothing found; nothing else in the save changed. Testing from the console: `__city` (sites, `cityGo`, `powerOn`, `openDoor`).
Factions (Nov 11, Part 4): reputation per faction (bag → Factions). Rootkin start neutral (won't attack unless you do), Farlight friendly, the rest hostile. Raise it with gifts, contracts, freeing their people (prisoners tied up in rival capitals) and killing their enemies; lose it by killing their people, burning their depot (fuel/molotov/grenade at the depot in their capital — they raid at 40% for ten days) or killing their named leader (disorder for six days, then a revenge raid). A ceasefire (60 scrap) holds a hostile faction off for 7 days; 75+ and one finished contract lets you ally (their fighters join you on raid nights, cheaper trade). Talk at the white flag outside each capital, to a leader in person, or on the Arc's radio desk (Q). Night raids only come from factions that are hostile right now — if nobody is, the night is quiet. Outposts (scout camps, road checkpoints) change hands every morning; clear one and raise Kestrel's banner — each sends 6 scrap + 2 meat home a day and makes raids a little smaller. Out on the roads: patrols between outposts, ambushes, and rival groups fighting each other.

Settlement (Nov 11, Part 5): bag → Settlement. Assign each survivor a job (in Kestrel); every morning their work lands in the Kestrel stores by the fire (take from it, or give from your bag — projects are paid from the stores). Prisoners you free (Part 4) turn up the next morning; six lone survivors (Millbrook, a pod camp, KVRN, the drover's camp, Route 9, the Long Fall) will come if Kestrel has a bed. Start one project at a time; builders finish it over a few days. Guards man the wall on raid nights, medics heal you while you're home (faster with the infirmary), repairers fix your worst bit of gear once a day when you open the Settlement tab in Kestrel.

Missions (Nov 11, Part 6): a mission comes to you (card + who's asking) and the objective line/marker follow it; you can wander off between steps. Rewards change the world: Clean Water puts 4 water in the stores every morning, Signal gives a radio warning at dusk of who's raiding, Taken/Missing bring people home, War Plans reveals the Night-100 coalition, and the later missions push you to turn clans, burn depots and dig in before Night 100.

Night 100 (Nov 11, Part 7): four stages announced as they come — The Approach, The Breach, The Taking, The Last Push. Keep the fires out (E at a burning building), treat the wounded, and kill anyone carrying a settler before they reach the trees. Your earlier choices set how many come and from where; allies show up for the last push. Win or lose, Kestrel goes on: the dead and taken are counted at dawn, burned buildings stay scorched until you rebuild them, and the Aftermath mission leads into Season 2 (the day counter becomes "of 200").

Events and side jobs (Nov 11, Part 8): out in the world, something turns up every few minutes (Old Hob the merchant, a distress call if you carry a radio, convoys, things falling from the sky, herds). The job board next to the stores has three side jobs a day (bag → Journal at the board); up to three at once, marked on the map. The Lantern: once you've got a radio (day 10+), the numbers lead to the relay, Survey Station 4 (breakers: the scratched note tells the order), the relay's dish (the survey terminal gives the settings) and finally the Black Door.

Combat (Nov 11, Part 9): C to crouch (quieter, harder to see; a blade from behind while crouched is a silent takedown). Gunshots carry — guards come to look. Bring a survivor along from bag → Settlement; Z (or tap their tag above your health) cycles follow / wait / defend / attack. Faction leaders fight like bosses: dodge Garrun's charge so he hits a tree, get behind Rask (or blast him) and step out of his fire, and watch your back with Kael.
